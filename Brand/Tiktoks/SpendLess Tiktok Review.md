Five things that helps me get over my shopping addiction,
1. Figuring our why I had a shopping addiction at all. For some people it's boredom, a vision of a better self, anxiety
2. It's not an all-or-nothing model. sometimes I'd buy one thing and heap the shame on myself and felt like the only way to cope was to spend more. One purchase doesn't have to mean a relapse. 
3. Using a budgeting app to help me track my shopping expenses. I like Monarch!
4. An app that help me block my shopping apps like SpendLess
5. I finally found a wishlist that helps me decide if I should make a purchase or put it off. It shows me everything I could want about a my list!
6. Building a shopping list that encourages me to think before I spend
7. Getting involved in the underconsumption core trend on TikTok. 
8. Seeing all the deinfluencers like X and Y reminding me that all of this is just stuff
9. Realizing that going cold turkey wasn't really an option and getting support didn't mean I was weak
10. I stopped viewing it as a hobby. It's nothing more than consummerism.
11. Build shopping lists of things that improve my life rather than things that look cute or fun
12. It's okay to use tools and apps! Willpower isn't enough
13. Realizing a sale isn't a good reason to buy anything. Needing it is and nothing else!
14. I stopped supporting fast fashion when I realized how bad it was for everyone involved. Second-hand only now (yes, sometimes even underwear)
15. Be purposeful when shopping. I shop for what I need. A pair of shoes. A new book. A replacement back-up. One and done.
16. We're done with wishlists. Companies just use them against us to get us shopping more. If I didn't buy it in the moment it wasn't worth it.
17. I don't actually "deserve it" that was just delusion I used t justify something I didn't need.
18. Get it through my head that an impulse buy IS AN IMPULSE. It happened because someone paid a lot of money is trying to trick my little monkey brain.
19. Always use a question checklist before buying; what happens if I wait a day or week before buying this? How often will I wear it? What's my cost per wear? Does buying this sign me up to purchase more things?
20. Watch whatever content I can about budgeting and psychology to learn how my brain is working
21. Social media is meant to push ads on us and get us to buy more. The more I cut out social media the less likely I was to make a purchase
22. Set small goals for myself. It doesn't just have to be about days not making purchases but the amount of times I wanted to buy, but didn't
23. Set up my big financial goals so I have something else to work toward. Every time I don't buy I'm actively working towards my goal. Replace the dopamine! 
24. Practice self-compassion. When I had a relapse I didn't beat myself up about it. I acknowledge that I made a mistake and made a commitment to do better.
25. Create a dopamine menu. I always shopped because I thought it was "fun", but I often just left with less money and some anxiety.  A dopamine menu made it easy to find other quick activities to do.
26. There is no such thing as scarcity. They're manipulating you. No company is dumb enough not to have the product they're advertising.
27. Sales are an advertisement. They don't go away they just trick you into thinking you got a good deal.
28. I have bought and regretted buying more items on impulse than those I haven't regretted at all. 
29. Realizing the difference between making a bad purchase and not is one deep breath and letting my brain reset
30. Money in my bank feels way better than a jacket I'll wear once (or never :cry:)
31. Companies care about their bottom line not if I'm down to my last $10 or how much debt I have. They will abandon me the second I don't spend. 
32. Delete payment information from every website and apple pay. It needs to be hard to buy something
33. Got more careful talking with my friends about wanting to buy something. They always would encourage "just buy it" even if it was obviously the wrong move. 
34. Calculated my REAL afer-tax and work expense hourly wage. I use that to see how much I have to work just to buy something.
35. Learned about no-buy months. Instead of trying to fix everything forever, just commit to one month and see what changes. Gave me time to pause and reflect. 
36. Learned about project plan really helped me understand how much I waste on products
37. Just calculated how much I could spend every month :cry: and how much I wasted over time!
